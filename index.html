THE TWO LOOPS

Pulmonary vs systemic circulation

Pulmonary circulation

Right ventricle → pulmonary arteries → lung capillaries → pulmonary veins → left atrium. Blood releases carbon dioxide and takes up oxygen.

Systemic circulation

Left ventricle → aorta → body arteries → tissue capillaries → veins → venae cavae → right atrium. Oxygen is delivered to tissues.

RED CELL CARGO

What changes along the route?

O₂
Oxygen loading

In lung capillaries, oxygen diffuses into blood and binds to haemoglobin.

CO₂
Carbon dioxide unloading

In lung capillaries, carbon dioxide moves from blood into the air spaces to be exhaled.

BLOOD ANATOMY

More than red cells.

Blood is a living tissue made of cells and a fluid matrix called plasma.

◉
ERYTHROCYTES

Red blood cells

Flexible, biconcave cells packed with haemoglobin. They transport oxygen and carry some carbon dioxide between lungs and tissues.

Key role · Gas transport
✺
LEUKOCYTES

White blood cells

A varied group of immune cells. Different types help recognize pathogens, coordinate immune responses and remove infected or damaged cells.

Key role · Defence
≈
PLASMA

Plasma

The pale-yellow liquid portion of blood. It carries water, electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, waste products and many proteins.

Key role · Transport medium
✣
PLATELETS

Platelets

Small cell fragments that help form a platelet plug and support the clotting process when a blood vessel is injured.

Key role · Haemostasis
IMMUNE MEMORY

Why a first exposure and a later exposure can differ

01 · First exposure

The immune system encounters an unfamiliar antigen. Some B cells that recognize it can become activated.

02 · Response develops

Activated B cells can form antibody-secreting plasma cells and memory B cells. The timing and strength vary.

03 · Later exposure

Memory cells can support a faster, stronger response. In Rh(D) sensitization, anti-D may matter in a later exposure to D-positive red cells.
