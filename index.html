03 / Reference library

Eight common blood groups

Explore the simplified antigen patterns used in this simulation.

Understanding the labels

The ABO letter describes A and/or B antigens on red blood cells. The plus or minus sign indicates whether the Rh(D) antigen is present in this simplified model. Blood group systems are more extensive than ABO and Rh(D).

04 / Learning modules

Blood science essentials

Core concepts behind the visual model.

🔴

Red blood cells

Red blood cells carry oxygen using hemoglobin. Their membranes display blood-group antigens, which can be recognized by antibodies.

🧬

Antigens

Antigens are molecular structures that can be recognized by the immune system. A, B, and D are examples used in this teaching model.

Y

Antibodies

Antibodies are proteins that bind specific targets. ABO antibodies are commonly present according to ABO group; anti-D can develop after exposure.

🩸

Plasma

Plasma is the liquid component of blood. It transports proteins, nutrients, hormones, and antibodies throughout the body.

🛡️

Immune recognition

When an antibody recognizes its matching antigen, binding may occur. In transfusion medicine, clinically significant reactions must be assessed by laboratory professionals.

🔬

Crossmatching

Clinical compatibility assessment includes more than the ABO/Rh(D) patterns shown here. Antibody screening and crossmatching help detect additional incompatibilities.

What this simulation does not model

Other blood-group antigens (such as Kell, Kidd, and Duffy), antibody strength, dosage effects, laboratory test results, patient history beyond the optional anti-D setting, component-specific rules, and clinical decision-making are not represented.
